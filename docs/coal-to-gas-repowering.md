# Repowering coal sites with natural gas

A coal plant can switch its existing boiler to gas, or it can retire the coal equipment and
repower the site with gas turbines. Electrify's two conversion actions represent repowering.
A peaker installs simple-cycle turbines. A combined-cycle project also recovers turbine exhaust
heat to generate steam and produce more electricity from the same fuel.

## When repowering became practical

- **Simple cycle.** Utility gas turbines entered service in Switzerland in 1939 and in the U.S.
  in 1949. U.S. utilities installed more than 45 GW of gas turbines from 1966 to 1976. Later
  aircraft-derived turbines offered fast starts and compact packages, and factory assembly
  reduced installation work. Existing coal sites made useful locations. At TVA's operating
  Johnsonville coal site, simple-cycle turbines installed in 1974 initially burned distillate
  because pipeline natural gas did not reach the site until 1999.
  [ASME, Neuchâtel turbine](https://www.asme.org/wwwasmeorg/media/resourcefiles/aboutasme/who%20we%20are/engineering%20history/landmarks/135-neuchatel-gas-turbine.pdf),
  [ASME, Belle Isle turbine](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/100-belle-isle-gas-turbine),
  [TVA, Johnsonville history](https://www.tva.com/Energy/Our-Power-System/Natural-Gas/Johnsonville-Combustion-Turbine-Plant).
- **Combined cycle.** Pre-engineered combined-cycle equipment was in commercial use by 1967.
  Higher-temperature, higher-output gas turbines and packaged heat recovery made larger
  projects practical. Virginia Power studied repowering two Chesterfield coal units in the
  1980s, and a new gas-fired GE 107F entered service there in 1990. Steam turbine reuse became
  a project-specific engineering choice. The existing steam path needs compatible pressure,
  temperature and flow, plus enough remaining life to warrant refurbishment.
  [ASME, Ottawa landmark](https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/283-ottawa-gas-turbine-combined-cycle-power-plant%2C-1967),
  [Virginia Power and Bechtel, 1988](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3240106),
  [Virginia DEQ, Chesterfield permit](https://www.deq.virginia.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/5939/638652796202400000),
  [EPRI, coal repowering](https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/000000003002025894/0/Product).
- **Widespread U.S. adoption.** Horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing helped expand
  U.S. shale gas supply in the 2000s. In 2011–2019, low gas prices, tighter air-emissions
  standards and more efficient turbines led to 103 switches from coal to gas in EIA's survey. Of these, 86
  converted existing boilers (14.3 GW) and 17 replaced coal plants with combined cycles. The
  latter projects installed 15.3 GW where 7.9 GW of coal capacity had stood. Those figures
  describe the projects EIA counted during this period.
  [EIA, shale gas production](https://www.eia.gov/TODAYINENERGY/detail.php?id=2170),
  [EIA, plant inventory analysis](https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=44636).

## Simple-cycle peaker

The project retires coal generation and installs new gas turbine-generators, intake and exhaust,
controls, air-pollution equipment and any gas conditioning and compression. A simple cycle
exhausts the hot gas after the combustion turbine. With no steam system to bring up to
temperature, it can respond quickly to short demand peaks. Modern aeroderivative machines can
reach full output in roughly ten minutes. Land, access roads, electrical service and
potentially usable interconnection equipment provide most of the reuse value. Gas supply and
switchgear can still require substantial work.
[TVA, aeroderivative startup](https://www.tva.com/the-powerhouse/stories/energy-on-demand),
[EPRI, site infrastructure and interconnection](https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/000000003002025894/0/Product).

- **Martin Drake, Colorado.** Six modular turbines went into space formerly used for the
  coal pile. The owner reported 162 MW for the six units. The last coal burn was August 2021,
  installation began in September 2021, the first unit generated during testing in March 2023
  and all six went into commercial service in May 2023. The installation-to-service clock was
  about 20 months. The vendor's two-week figure refers to setting up a factory-assembled
  package under favorable conditions.
  [Colorado Springs Utilities, board update](https://www.csu.org/hubfs/Utilities-Board/Agendas/3-16-2022-Utilities-Board-Agenda-March-2022.pdf),
  [Colorado Springs Utilities, commissioning](https://www.csu.org/blog/natual-gas-units-to-be-commissioned-by-may-2023),
  [GE Vernova, project case study](https://www.gevernova.com/gas-power/resources/case-studies/colorado-springs-coal-to-gas-conversion).
- **Johnsonville, Tennessee.** A larger installation at the retired coal site added ten
  aeroderivative peakers with 530 MW of summer net capability. Construction followed
  demolition in 2022, nine units entered commercial service in May 2025 and the final unit in
  August. TVA reported $646 million spent with $6 million still expected in its FY2025 filing,
  or about $1,230/kW at the reported summer output. The plant needed new foundations, a
  dedicated switchyard and a control room despite the existing power site.
  [TVA, construction account](https://www.tva.com/the-powerhouse/stories/johnsonville-s-jet-set),
  [TVA FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1376986/000137698625000056/tve-20250930.htm).

## Combined-cycle repowering

The project installs one or more gas turbines and heat-recovery steam generators (HRSGs).
Steam from the HRSGs drives a second turbine. Where appropriate, the project retains the coal
station's steam turbine and generator, condenser, cooling system or water infrastructure.
A full replacement installs a matched new steam cycle and may be built adjacent to the operating
coal plant. Steam system integration, cooling and testing generally add work relative to a peaker.
Direct connection to the grid also depends on interconnection capacity and approval. Both
paths need sufficient pipeline delivery pressure and flow, even at peak demand, and a new
air-permit review can apply. Coal ash closure and remediation are separate site obligations.
[National Petroleum Council, repowering configurations](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/4-5_Policy_Options_Gas_GHG_Reduction_Technologies.pdf),
[EPRI, assets and permitting](https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/000000003002025894/0/Product).

- **Big Bend Unit 1, Florida.** Tampa Electric refurbished the steam turbine and generator,
  kept once-through cooling and installed two gas turbines and HRSGs. Design began in 2017
  and field work in 2019. The gas turbines entered service in simple-cycle mode in 2021 and
  the combined cycle entered service in December 2022. The utility filed an actual cost of
  $875 million for the full modernization project, which included retiring Unit 2. Dividing
  the total by Unit 1's 1,055 MW nominal summer capacity gives about $830/kW. The full
  construction schedule included a period when the gas turbines could already supply power.
  [Tampa Electric testimony, printed pp. 16–17](https://www.floridapsc.com/library/filings/2024/09275-2024/09275-2024.pdf).
- **Plant Morrow, Mississippi.** Cooperative Energy announced the conversion in June 2018.
  Its two 200 MW coal units retired that November. The project reused one steam turbine
  with a new advanced-class gas turbine and reached commercial operation in March 2023 at
  572 MW. That was about four years and four months from coal retirement. The utility's
  announcement identifies savings from using in-house labor, and MISO separately reports a
  $15.9 million estimate for supporting transmission and substation work. These figures
  describe specific cost components.
  [Cooperative Energy, commissioning](https://cooperativeenergy.com/cooperative-energys-plant-morrow-enters-new-era/),
  [Siemens Energy, project case study](https://www.siemensenergy.com/us/en/home/references/cooperative-energy-morrow.html),
  [MISO, interconnection project](https://cdn.misoenergy.org/20230906%20SSPM3%20Item%2003c-3%20Mississippi%20Proposed%20Projects630067.pdf).

## Project lead times

EIA's greenfield engineering references allow 40 months for a heavy-duty peaker and 42 for a
combined cycle, including 18 months of development, permitting and engineering in both cases.
The repowering examples span about 20 months to three years for peaker field work and about three
to four years for combined-cycle field work or a post-retirement transition. The start points
differ by project. Site prep and testing take time even when a package arrives mostly assembled.
[EIA/Sargent & Lundy, Tables 4-1 and 5-1](https://www.eia.gov/analysis/studies/powerplants/capitalcost/pdf/capital_cost_AEO2025.pdf).

## Which cost goes in the denominator?

The suggested 15–30% is plausible when comparing a new heavy-duty peaker with a new coal
plant, or when comparing certain boiler-only contracts with new gas capacity. Neither
comparison prices a peaker repower as a share of a comparable new peaker.

- EIA's new-build engineering cases in 2023 dollars are **$836/kW** for a 419 MW heavy-duty
  simple-cycle unit, **$1,606/kW** for a 211 MW aeroderivative peaker installation,
  **$868/kW** for a 1,227 MW combined cycle and **$4,103/kW** for a 650 MW coal plant.
  The heavy-duty peaker is about **20% of new coal capital per kW**. The gas cases differ in
  size and scope. An actual project budget cannot establish the discount against building
  the same facility on a different site.
  [EIA/Sargent & Lundy, AEO2025 engineering cases, Table 1-1](https://www.eia.gov/analysis/studies/powerplants/capitalcost/pdf/capital_cost_AEO2025.pdf).
- An engineering study cited by the National Petroleum Council estimated about **25% savings**
  for brownfield CC repowering versus greenfield CC construction. The site still pays for
  major new generating equipment. The evidence above supports budgeting most of the price
  of a comparable new peaker for simple-cycle repowering. The exact benefit depends on
  what the project can reuse and what it needs to remediate or upgrade.
  [National Petroleum Council, appendix D](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/4-5_Policy_Options_Gas_GHG_Reduction_Technologies.pdf).
- A **boiler fuel switch** retains the steam plant. It installs gas burners, piping and
  controls, and may modify boiler heat-transfer surfaces or derate the unit. The EPA used an
  average of about $52/kW for the boiler modifications alone and estimated gas pipeline
  work separately. In 2024, Babcock & Wilcox disclosed a $246 million boiler conversion
  contract at Petersburg for 1,160 MW, about $212/kW of coal capacity. That contract is
  roughly 25% of EIA's new large peaker cost per kW, using the published amounts without
  inflation adjustment. It buys a gas-fired steam plant with the old steam cycle, different
  efficiency and slower startup than a turbine peaker. NRG converted two existing steam
  units at Arthur Kill this way in the early 1990s. An industry stakeholder interviewed by GAO
  in 2008 estimated four to six months offline for boiler modification.
  [EPA, published engineering cost assumptions](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/24/24A117/321093/20240729172037439_GHG%20App.%20Appendix.pdf),
  [Babcock & Wilcox, Petersburg contract](https://www.babcock.com/home/about/corporate/news/babcock-and-wilcox-receives-full-notice-to-proceed-on-246-million-contract-for-indiana-power-plant-coal-to-gas-fuel-switching-project),
  [NRG Form 10-K, Arthur Kill](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1013871/000095012310015824/y81314e10vk.htm),
  [GAO, fuel-switching interviews, p. 15](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-08-601r.pdf).

## Operating implications

The EIA reference peaker takes 9.142 MMBtu/MWh, the combined cycle takes 6.266 and the new
coal case takes 8.638 at rated conditions. Multiplying those heat rates by EIA's fuel CO₂
coefficients yields roughly 484 kg of direct CO₂ per MWh for the peaker, 332 for combined cycle
and 829 for coal. These are representative full-load combustion rates. Cycling, actual running
hours and upstream methane affect net emissions. Gas avoids coal ash and substantially reduces
sulfur and particulate pollution during operation, and gas turbine emissions still include NOₓ.
[EIA/Sargent & Lundy, technical cases](https://www.eia.gov/analysis/studies/powerplants/capitalcost/pdf/capital_cost_AEO2025.pdf),
[EIA, fuel CO₂ factors](https://www.eia.gov/environment/emissions/co2_vol_mass.php).

## Recommendation for Electrify

- **Convert to Natural Gas Peaker.** Price at 85% of a normal peaker new-build quote for
  the resulting capacity and allow about 18 months of project time before difficulty scaling.
  The price represents a 15% site reuse saving and the time approximates a fast modular
  project. The game enables peaker repowers from 1966, when U.S. deployment was scaling up.
- **Convert to Natural Gas CC.** Price at 75% of a normal combined-cycle quote for the
  resulting capacity and allow about 36 months before difficulty scaling. The price reflects
  the engineering estimate for brownfield savings and the time approximates field construction
  at Big Bend. Use the game's existing 1990 combined-cycle availability.
- Size the replacement to the coal nameplate rating, capped at the destination technology's site
  maximum. Actual projects choose output from turbine sizes, site conditions and system
  needs. A fixed equal-size conversion gives players a predictable quote and keeps incremental
  capacity a separate decision. Apply normal gas dispatch, fuel, costs and emissions once the
  new equipment is commissioned. Start a fresh gas-facility service life at commissioning,
  consistent with modeling replacement generating equipment. A real burner retrofit keeps
  the aging steam plant, and a combined cycle that retains old machinery must assess its
  remaining life individually. Assume the site can obtain gas service within the project scope.
- Allow normal coal operation during neighboring construction and commission the replacement
  at a single point. Real sites can schedule shutdowns and grid tie-ins at different points.
  Plants that reuse the steam turbine can have a substantial gap, as Morrow did, while
  independent units can be commissioned in phases, as at Big Bend. The game project
  duration models construction. Coal still reaching an independent outage or retirement date
  follows the game's normal service-life rules.

The cost fractions and project durations above are representative game settings drawn from
case evidence. Published examples vary by site, project scope, permitting and the condition
of the equipment available for reuse.

In-game conversions are cash purchases funded at approval. The paid project counts as an
asset during construction. Commissioning retires the old coal equipment's remaining resale
value and starts depreciating the new gas investment. The site keeps its dispatch position,
operating history and any outstanding debt. Canceling before commissioning refunds the purchase
price. The gas construction emissions accrue over the project's actual elapsed work at the
catalog's rate for the destination technology. The game keeps guided missions and coal
facilities with transfer restrictions or mandated disconnections on their authored paths.
