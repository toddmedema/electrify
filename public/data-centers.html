Can your grid keep up?

Put yourself in charge of your local power supply. Use Electrify to explore how a wave of data-center demand changes the balance between reliable electricity, affordable power, and lower emissions.

Start with a grid close to home

Choose your city or a nearby available location in Custom setup. Set the starting year, customer count, and starting power plants to approximate the grid you want to explore.

Keep Data Center Boom selected under Scenario events. The event adds a scheduled increase in data-center demand, scaled to your custom grid, with time to prepare before it connects. Setup extends the duration to cover the event so you can see its aftermath.

Check the Year 1 outlook, then press Play. Expand generation, add storage, and explore demand-management policies as your grid grows.

Run the comparison

Play once with Data Center Boom and once with it deselected. Keep your location, starting fleet, other settings, and random seed the same so the added event is the difference you are exploring.

Then try a different response: build capacity earlier, change the generation mix, or reduce peak demand. Compare the results in Insights and your end-of-game review.

What would you change?

  • Reliability: Can supply cover the extra demand through the day and across seasons? When do shortages appear?
  • Costs: How do new construction, fuel purchases, and electricity rates affect your utility's finances and customers?
  • Emissions: How does meeting the new load with different combinations of generation and storage change your grid's footprint?
  • Timing: What happens if you wait until the data centers arrive to start building?

Understand the tradeoffs behind the headlines

Electrify is a research-backed electricity-market game. Its local weather and regional assumptions help you explore different places, but your custom grid is an approximation, not a reconstruction of your utility's network or a forecast for a specific proposed campus.

Data Center Boom uses an authored demand schedule scaled to your settings; it does not import a project's requested megawatts or connection date. Use the results to develop questions about capacity, timing, costs, and emissions in your community. Learn more about Electrify or explore the model and its sources.
