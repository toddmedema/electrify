Data centers and your community

Can your grid keep up?

A new data center needs power around the clock. Where will it come from, what will it cost, and who has to plan for it? Run an example grid in Electrify and see what it takes to supply that extra electricity.

An example: adding 100 MW

We ran Electrify’s Austin example twice, starting in 2026 with 575,087 customer accounts. In one run, no new campus opened. In the other, a 100 MW campus opened in 2027. We left the same plants, electricity prices, and customer programs in place in both runs. Here is what happened during 2027.

Modeled result in 2027 No new campus 100 MW campus
Smallest spare supply No new campus682 MW 100 MW campus582 MW
Fuel and running costs No new campus$448.3 million 100 MW campus$459.3 million
Emissions No new campus6.145 million tonnes CO₂e 100 MW campus6.313 million tonnes CO₂e

The grid supplied all its customers in both runs. The new campus used some of its spare supply and added about $11 million in fuel and running costs, along with 168,000 tonnes CO₂e in emissions.

Try it yourself

  1. Open the data-center setup. Choose Austin, set Start year to 2026 and Data centers open to 2027, and keep Grid size at 575,087 accounts.
  2. Under How much extra power?, set 0 MW and choose Start exploring. Leave plants, prices, and customer programs unchanged. Watch Insights through the end of 2027.
  3. Return to the setup and repeat with 100 MW, keeping everything else the same. Then try your own response: build generation, add storage, or reduce other demand.

Zero added demand keeps existing customers, including existing data centers. The new campus draws constant power, while the game uses one representative day per month. This exercise cannot forecast a real project’s reliability or local bills.

Take these questions to your community

  • Which power plants and grid upgrades must be ready when the campus opens?
  • Who will pay for the new infrastructure and ongoing electricity use?
  • What supplies power on a still night, and how does a clean-energy promise account for those hours?
