Electrify vs En-ROADS

In Electrify, you run one utility and decide how to meet tonight’s demand. In En-ROADS, you try climate policies and see how they could change global temperatures by 2100.

Which question are you asking?

How do I keep the lights on?

Try Electrify. Suppose the sun is setting, demand is rising, and your new power plant is still under construction. Can you cover the shortfall with storage or imports? What will that do to your utility’s finances?

How could we limit global warming?

Try En-ROADS. What happens to warming if cars and buildings switch to electricity? Try the same changes with a cleaner power supply, then see what forests, agriculture, or carbon removal could contribute.

What each model can show

Both tools model storage. In Electrify, a battery has a maximum power output and a limited charge. It can run flat while your customers still need electricity. En-ROADS models how much storage a growing wind and solar fleet needs and what it costs. Its storage assumptions affect renewable adoption through cost. The model sets no direct limit on renewable use from storage.

In Electrify, you can add a data center to a local grid and see how much spare power you have when it opens. En-ROADS models data-center demand worldwide. Its data-center documentation explains that local grid constraints, siting, and local electricity prices are outside its scope.

Electrify tracks your utility’s emissions. To explore changes in global temperature, use En-ROADS. Its baseline scenario gives you a starting point for comparing policies.

Try both with a class

Start in En-ROADS. Electrify more transport and buildings, then see what happens when you add cleaner electricity. Switch to Electrify and try to supply a growing city with lower-emission power. Where did you need another plant or more storage? Bring that question back to the class.

The En-ROADS dynamics guide explains why cleaner electricity and electrification work together. The tools run independently. Results from one don’t carry over to the other.

Common questions

Which simulator is more accurate?

It depends on what you want to measure. Electrify can tell you when your simulated grid runs short of power. En-ROADS can estimate warming under a set of global policies. Each result depends on the assumptions in that model.

Are Electrify and En-ROADS affiliated?

No. Electrify is an independent project. You can read more about En-ROADS in its official En-ROADS overview.
