Find the right energy simulator

Want to run a utility, explore climate solutions, or test an energy policy? These four tools answer different questions. Start with the one closest to yours.

Compare the tools Play Electrify

What would you like to explore?

Tool Your question Scale and format
Electrify Your questionCan I keep the lights on while cutting emissions and paying the bills? Scale and formatA utility in a selected location. Build plants, set prices, and manage costs in a free browser game for computers and phones.
En-ROADS Your questionWhich combinations of climate solutions could limit global warming? Scale and formatGlobal energy, land, economy, and climate. Change policies and assumptions, then compare possible futures through 2100.
Energy Transition Model Your questionWhat could a country, region, or city's future energy system look like? Scale and formatA selected area and future year. Try changes to energy supply, demand, and storage, and inspect their effects on costs and emissions.
Energy Policy Simulator Your questionHow could a policy package affect emissions, the economy, and public health? Scale and formatAvailable country and regional models. Test policies such as carbon pricing or efficiency standards across electricity, transport, buildings, and other sectors.

We make Electrify and wrote this guide to help you choose. We’re independent of the other projects; this comparison does not imply their endorsement.

Know what each result can tell you

Select a tool’s name above to open it. For Energy Transition Model and Energy Policy Simulator, check that a region relevant to your question is available.

Read results alongside each model’s geography, time periods, and assumptions. Electrify uses one representative day per month and tracks emissions without calculating global temperature. Our En-ROADS comparison explains how a game about running a utility can complement global climate exploration.

For the other tools’ assumptions and tutorials, read the En-ROADS user guide, Energy Transition Model documentation, and Energy Policy Simulator documentation. Teachers can also try our classroom activity.
