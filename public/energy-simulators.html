Four tools for four questions

Tool Your question Scale and format
Electrify Your questionCan I keep the lights on while cutting emissions and paying the bills? Scale and formatRun a utility in the location you choose. Build plants, set prices, and manage costs in a free browser game for computers and phones.
En-ROADS Your questionWhich combinations of climate solutions could limit global warming? Scale and formatGlobal energy, land, economy, and climate. Change policies and assumptions, then compare possible futures through 2100.
Energy Transition Model Your questionWhat could a country, region, or city's future energy system look like? Scale and formatA selected area and future year. Try changes to energy supply, demand, and storage, and inspect their effects on costs and emissions.
Energy Policy Simulator Your questionHow could a policy package affect emissions, the economy, and public health? Scale and formatAvailable country and regional models. Test policies such as carbon pricing or efficiency standards across electricity, transport, buildings, and other sectors.

We make Electrify. The other projects are independent, and listing them here doesn’t imply their endorsement.

Choosing a model

If you’re studying a particular country or region, check whether Energy Transition Model or Energy Policy Simulator has a model for it.

To try running a utility through a cold snap or a spike in demand, play an Electrify scenario. The game tracks your grid’s emissions. For questions about temperature worldwide, see how En-ROADS and Electrify compare.

For the other tools’ assumptions and tutorials, read the En-ROADS user guide, Energy Transition Model documentation, and Energy Policy Simulator documentation. For a lesson on keeping a grid running after sunset, use the Electrify classroom guide.
