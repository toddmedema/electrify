Build a grid you can afford to keep running

Electrify is a free power grid simulator game about running an electric utility. You choose power plants and storage, set electricity prices, and decide when to borrow for the next project. Your job is to supply customers reliably while managing costs and emissions. It runs in your browser on a computer, tablet, or phone, and you can start playing without an account.

The game uses research-backed electricity data and scenarios inspired by real places and energy challenges. It makes the tradeoffs visible: a cheap plant may need expensive fuel, and a clean grid still needs enough electricity when demand peaks. It is a strategy game for exploring those decisions, rather than an engineering tool for designing electrical circuits or planning an actual utility.

How to play in four steps

  1. Learn the controls. Choose Play and begin with the tutorials. Start small, watch a day unfold, and learn where to find your power plants, forecasts, and finances. Pause whenever you want time to think.
  2. Choose your challenge. Open an official scenario or create a custom game. Read the starting situation and goals before spending your budget. Different places need different plans.
  3. Build for the hours ahead. Compare construction time, running costs, and output. Check the forecast before committing: a power plant under construction cannot help with tonight’s shortage.
  4. Watch, adjust, and try again. Follow electricity supply and demand as time advances. Inspect a shortage or a costly day, change your approach, and see whether the next day improves. Speed time up when you are ready.

What does a power grid simulator teach?

Supply and demand change together. Your customers need electricity throughout the day, and extreme weather can push demand higher just when parts of the fleet struggle. Enough annual generation does not guarantee enough power at the hardest hour.

Different generators do different jobs. Wind and solar output depend on the weather and time of day. Dispatchable plants offer control, but come with their own fuel, operating, and construction costs. Storage shifts energy between hours: a battery can cover a shortage only if it has enough charge.

Reliability, costs, and emissions interact. Changing your electricity price affects customers and revenue. Borrowing can bring a project forward, but creates future payments. Replacing fossil generation can cut emissions while changing how you cover the evening peak. Try a mix, inspect the results, and compare another approach.

Put your plan through a real-world challenge

Try the Deep Freeze scenario to prepare for a severe cold snap, or the Heatwave + Drought scenario to face hot weather and limited water. In Delhi Summer, growing demand meets summer heat. The Data Center Boom scenario asks how you would supply a wave of new customers. For a longer view, explore the Legacy campaign’s First Light chapter and carry one utility across generations.

Questions before you play

Is Electrify free?

Yes. You can play the browser game for free. You do not need an account to begin the tutorials or try a scenario.

Do I need to download anything?

No download is needed for the browser game. Open Electrify, choose Play, and start with the tutorials. A larger screen gives the charts more room, but the game also works on phones.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. The classroom lesson and printable worksheet give teachers a starting activity and discussion prompts. Students can compare plans and explain which tradeoffs changed their results.

How is it different from a climate simulator?

Electrify puts you in charge of one utility’s day-to-day choices. Broader tools explore whole energy systems or global policy. Compare energy and climate simulators, or read the Electrify and En-ROADS comparison to choose a tool for your question.
