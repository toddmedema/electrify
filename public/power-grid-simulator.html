Build a grid you can afford to keep running

In Electrify, you run an electric utility. You choose power plants and storage, set electricity prices, and decide when to borrow for the next project. Your customers count on you to keep the lights on, even when a heat wave hits or fuel gets expensive. You can play in a browser on your computer, tablet, or phone without an account.

The game draws on electricity research and real places. A plant that’s cheap to build may cost you a lot in fuel. Solar can help you cut that bill, but you’ll need a plan for the evening peak. The model simplifies each month to one representative day so you can play through years of decisions.

How to play in four steps

  1. Learn the controls. Choose Play and begin with the tutorials. Watch a day unfold and find your power plants, forecasts, and finances. Pause whenever you want time to think.
  2. Choose your challenge. Open an official scenario or create a custom game. Read the starting situation and goals before spending your budget. What does this grid already have, and when will it need more power?
  3. Build for the hours ahead. Compare construction time, running costs, and output. Check the forecast before you buy. A plant under construction won’t help with tonight’s shortage.
  4. Watch, adjust, and try again. Follow electricity supply and demand as time advances. If the grid runs short, pause and look at when demand overtook supply. Try a change and speed up time when you’re ready.

Reading your grid

Timing matters. In extreme weather your customers may need more electricity just when some plants struggle to deliver it. A grid can produce plenty of electricity over the year and still come up short at the busiest hour.

Different generators do different jobs. Wind and solar output depend on the weather and time of day. You can call on a gas plant when you need it, but you’ll pay for the plant, its upkeep, and its fuel. A battery can cover an evening shortage if there was enough spare power to charge it earlier.

Reliability, costs, and emissions interact. Changing your electricity price affects customers and revenue. Borrowing can bring a project forward, but creates future payments. Replacing fossil generation can cut emissions while changing how you cover the evening peak.

Pick a grid to run

Try the Deep Freeze scenario to prepare for a severe cold snap, or the Heatwave + Drought scenario to face hot weather and limited water. In Delhi Summer, growing demand meets summer heat. The Data Center Boom scenario asks how you would supply a wave of new customers. For a longer view, explore the Legacy campaign’s First Light chapter and carry one utility across generations.

Questions before you play

Is Electrify free?

Yes. The browser game is free, and you can start the tutorials or play a scenario without an account.

Do I need to download anything?

Just open Electrify in your browser, choose Play, and start with the tutorials. A larger screen gives the charts more room, but the game also works on phones.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. The classroom guide includes a 45-minute lesson for grades 9–12, a printable student worksheet, and connections to NGSS and AP Environmental Science.

How is it different from a climate simulator?

Electrify tracks the power your utility produces and the emissions that come with it. Climate simulators can use global policies to estimate how much the planet will warm. Compare energy and climate simulators, or read the Electrify and En-ROADS comparison.
