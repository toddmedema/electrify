Run your own power grid

The sun is going down and demand is climbing. Will your grid get through the evening? In Electrify, you’re in charge of the power plants, the electricity price, and the next decision.

Build a grid you can afford to keep running

Choose power plants and storage, set electricity prices, and decide when to borrow for the next project. A cheap plant may need expensive fuel. A wind farm’s output changes with the weather. A battery can cover a shortage, if you charged it in time.

Keep the lights on

Match supply to demand as the weather changes and customers use more power. Check the forecast before a shortage, leave time for construction, and use storage or connections to neighboring grids where available.

Keep the business running

Pay for fuel, upkeep, and loans while building a cleaner fleet. Electricity prices affect both your income and your customers, so you need a plan for paying the bills as well as meeting demand.

Start with one day. Then take on a bigger grid.

Electrify runs in your browser on a computer, tablet, or phone. It is free to play, and you can start without an account.

  1. Learn the controls. Choose Play on the home screen. New players start with a short lesson on electricity supply and demand.
  2. Build your first plants. The next lessons introduce generators, storage, prices, and forecasts. You make the purchases and watch the results.
  3. Choose your challenge. Take on one of the scenarios, or create your own game by choosing a location and starting conditions.

You can pause to inspect the grid and speed time up when you are ready. The challenge is planning ahead: a plant under construction cannot help with tonight’s shortage.

A game about real tradeoffs

Electrify brings together weather, fuel costs, construction delays, customer demand, and emissions. It gives you room to try a plan, find its weak spot, and try again.

Each simulated day stands in for a month, and the clock advances in 15-minute steps. That keeps years of decisions playable, but leaves out much of a real utility’s day-to-day operation. A successful run cannot prove year-round reliability or predict a particular project’s costs. Electrify tracks emissions without calculating global temperature.
